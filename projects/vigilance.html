Research · Human and Systems Lab

Predicting Vigilance in Medical Residents

Duration
Sep 2023 – Present
Lab
Human and Systems Lab, UW · Dr. Ji-Eun Kim
Sensors
Tobii eye tracker · Empatica wristband
Methods
Field studies · Controlled experiments · Dynamic Bayesian networks

The question

How do medical residents' vigilance levels (their ability to sustain attention) change over time depending on the amount and quality of sleep they've had, and can we predict those changes?

My role

As a research assistant in the University of Washington's Human and Systems Lab under Dr. Ji-Eun Kim, I managed communications and scheduling with medical residents, and designed and ran experimental sessions. We used a Tobii eye tracker and an Empatica wristband to collect eye movement and physiological data from participants.

Modeling

After data collection, I built a dynamic Bayesian network that predicts vigilance levels from the collected sensor data, and I've contributed to multiple academic papers on our findings.

Why it matters

This data will someday contribute to an intervention that helps residents maintain vigilance during long shifts, supporting them in providing the best possible care to their patients.
