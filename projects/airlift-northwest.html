The problem

Nurses at Airlift Northwest carry transfusable blood on every flight. The total time that blood has spent out of refrigeration is a crucial metric for making sure it is safe for patients, so it has to be logged accurately, even in a fast-moving flight environment.

Goal

Design and user test a high-fidelity prototype of a digitized blood logging system for Airlift Northwest and UW Medicine.

Process

Our team reviewed the existing blood acquisition, storage, and delivery system and made recommendations to make removing blood from storage more intuitive, accurate, and efficient. I led the survey design and user interviews with nurses, and we used that feedback to shape a digital interface to replace the existing process.

Results

We designed and user tested a high-fidelity prototype that made logging time out of the fridge 71% faster and could save Airlift Northwest over $100,000 per year. Our team was evaluated by judges from industry and awarded first place in the Industrial and Systems Engineering Capstone Competition among 15 teams.
